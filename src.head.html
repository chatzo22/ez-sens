Train

Pick a drill. Click the arena to lock the mouse; Esc pauses. Every raw mouse count turns the camera by exactly yaw × sens of your setup, applied the instant the event arrives — independent of frame rate.

The trainer needs a desktop mouse (Chrome or Edge). The converter works fine on this device.

Flash dodge

Practice turning away from a flash in simple, original blockout maps inspired by common CS2 and Valorant situations (not copies of those maps). Use your current game sensitivity and crosshair. A photosensitivity notice appears before your first run; turn on Reduced flash intensity for a milder white-out. Only one flash plays at a time, and each fade lasts at least 300 ms. After each pop, simple bot targets (a capsule body and a sphere head) push out from cover — some take wide, fast swing peeks or shoulder-peek, and a mix stay standing, crouch, or switch stance mid-push — and you shoot them before they retreat. A full or partial blind makes them harder to see. Pick a damage model and bot colors in the options.

This drill needs a desktop mouse (Chrome or Edge).

Sensitivity finder

One warm-up, then five short rounds (10 flicks + 8 s tracking each) at different 360° distances around yours. Speeds are hidden and shuffled so you can't bias yourself. The finder fits a curve to your scores and nudges the result by your overshoot / undershoot tendency. Pick Retro Horizon (the default), Sunrise, Day or Night for the arena. The Range keeps its own look unless you choose Retro Horizon in Range settings. Frame-rate and quality settings from the Train tab apply here too.

The finder needs a desktop mouse (Chrome or Edge).

About EZ Sens

An original, independent tool. Not affiliated with Valve, Embark Studios or any game publisher. Runs 100% in your browser: no server, no account, no network calls during play.

The math

degrees per count = yaw × sens · cm/360 = 914.4 ÷ (DPI × degrees per count) (914.4 = 360° × 2.54 cm/in) · eDPI = DPI × sens. Converting keeps cm/360 the same. A few games scale with FOV or use an offset; those use their own formula below.

Status labels

  • VERIFIED engine default value that every source agrees on.
  • CONSENSUS not published by the developer, but every converter we checked uses the same value.
  • COMMUNITY-MEASURED measured by players / converter sites; sources agree within ~0.5%. Check with one 360° turn.
  • DISPUTED sources disagree; we use the best-supported value and show the alternative. Check with one 360° turn or calibrate it as a custom game.

Frame rate & latency

  • Everything — including the 3D engine (three.js r160, MIT, bundled in this file) — runs locally. Nothing is downloaded during play, so there is no "ping".
  • Rendering uses requestAnimationFrame, so it runs at your monitor's refresh rate (60/100/144/240 Hz+). The frame-rate setting can cap it lower; it can't go above the display's refresh rate (browsers sync to the display).
  • Mouse input uses pointer lock with unadjustedMovement (raw counts, no OS acceleration) and Chrome's pointerrawupdate events where available. Each event rotates the camera immediately; there is no smoothing or frame-time scaling, so sensitivity is identical at any FPS. Clicks are judged against the aim at the moment of the click.
  • Low graphics turns off antialiasing, fog and the film-grain overlay, and caps pixel ratio. Auto quality (on by default) does the same temporarily if frame time stays high. Render scale lowers resolution for weaker PCs.

How scoring works

  • Flick / micro: score = 100 × accuracy × (500 ms ÷ avg time-to-kill), capped. Overshoot / undershoot is judged where your crosshair first settles after each flick.
  • Tracking: score = % of time on target. Ahead of the target's motion = overshoot, behind = undershoot.
  • Finder: quadratic fit of score vs log(cm/360), clamped to the tested range, then up to ±6% slower if you overshoot / faster if you undershoot.
  • Flash dodge: play a situation (Long doors (Dust 2-style), choke hold, site anchor, site entry, off-angle retake, over-wall pop, mid-window cross, ramp walk, or a random mix). Maps are original blockouts inspired by common CS2 and Valorant flash habits, not copies of those maps. Each pop is scored dodged (turned past 90°), partial, full, or covered (fully behind a wall). After the pop, original capsule-and-sphere bots push out from cover into the open (some strafe or shoulder-peek; a Mix stance option — the default — keeps about 40% standing, 30% crouched and 30% switching mid-push, and groups of two or three always include both a standing bot and a crouched or switching one; Standing only, Crouch only and Switching only are also available). Hitboxes follow stance changes. The same Bot stance option applies to Train and Range bot targets. A blind makes them harder to see. Recap tracks dodge %, bots killed, moving kills, standing vs. crouch kills, time to first hit, accuracy, headshot %, average shots-to-kill and TTK (including average TTK on pushing bots). A damage-model option uses headshots only, or close-range Vandal / AK-47 values with or without armor. A Random throws & timing option (on by default) varies the gap between flashes, the throw or pop origin, the arc and the fuse (CS2-style about 1.2–2.0 s, Valorant-style about 0.25–0.6 s), while still leaving a dodge window at or above the difficulty minimum; turn it off to practice the authored variants. Timing presets are Valorant-style (short pop) or CS2-style (thrown bounce). Score rewards clean dodges, faster reactions and re-peek hits.

Bots and damage models

  • Optional Train / Range target type (Flash peeks always use it): an original low-poly bot — a capsule (pill) body and a sphere head. Default colors are a white body and a cyan head; both are adjustable. Range keeps its existing arena look; only the target mesh changes. These are not Aim Lab or other third-party assets.
  • Headshots only: only the head sphere counts; a body or leg hit is a miss.
  • Valorant, Vandal (close range, no falloff): 160 head, 40 body, 34 legs. No armor is 100 HP (1 / 3 / 3 shots to kill). Armor is heavy shields (50), absorbed one-for-one, for 150 effective HP (1 / 4 / 5). Sources: VALORANT Wiki — Vandal, valdb.gg.
  • CS2, AK-47 (point-blank; the trainer does not apply distance falloff): 144 head, 36 chest (body), 27 legs. Armor is kevlar + helmet (100 armor, 77.5% armor penetration). Helmeted head 111 (still a one-tap), kevlar body 27 (4 shots), legs are never armored (4 shots). Unarmored body is 3 shots. Sources: OP.GG AK-47, SteamAnalyst.
  • Bots start at 100 HP plus any armor and die when HP reaches 0. A short damage / hit-zone label pops on each shot. Recaps include headshot %, average shots-to-kill and time-to-kill when bots are in use.

Limitations

  • If the browser refuses unadjustedMovement, Windows pointer speed / "Enhance pointer precision" can affect the trainer (the HUD warns you). Set Windows to 6/11 with acceleration off.
  • Only hip-fire is converted. Vertical sens is treated as equal to horizontal. Rainbow Six Siege assumes the default MouseSensitivityMultiplierUnit 0.02; Battlefield 6 assumes Uniform Soldier Aiming on.
  • Menu rounding is only applied where we know the game's step (THE FINALS whole numbers; ARC Raiders whole numbers to be safe). Other games show the exact value — type the closest the game accepts.
  • Arena FOV only changes what you see, not turn distance. Third-person games (ARC Raiders, Deadlock) are trained in first-person.
  • A recommendation from one session is noisy. Run the finder 2–3 times on different days and average.
  • Appearance: Dark (default) and Light themes the whole UI. The 3D arenas are unchanged. The Sensitivity Finder, Train and Flash share arena environments: Retro Horizon (the default — a dark synthwave sky, glowing pink horizon and neon grid floor, with neon trim on Flash map edges), plus Sunrise, Day and Night (Night is the original dark arena). The Range keeps its own look unless you pick Retro Horizon in Range settings. Both choices are remembered in this browser.
  • Flash dodge: situational blockout maps with scripted throws, wall bounces, line-of-sight occlusion and post-flash peek bots. Some scenarios auto-walk you down a path (aim only); optional WASD movement is in the Flash options. Valorant-style uses a short on-screen wind-up; CS2-style uses a thrown bounce and a longer fuse. A photosensitivity notice is a fixed full-screen dialog shown once. Reduced flash intensity caps the white-out; flashes never overlap and each fade is at least 300 ms. A short on-screen toast reports Dodged, Partial, Flashed or Covered after each pop. Shots are blocked by map geometry (no wall-bangs). A tinnitus-style ring can be muted or turned down. Maps use simple original trim, frames, crates and fixtures; peek bots start in cover with a visible head and push into the open, and some crouch.
  • Off-screen hint: when a target is out of view, a red-to-magenta glow on the screen edge points at it and follows it as you turn (inspired by directional hit indicators, drawn from scratch). Toggle it in Train settings or the Range panel. Tracking targets stay above the floor. The glow tint shifts slightly with the time of day so it stays visible.
  • Results: after each Train round a recap shows score, accuracy, time-to-hit, first-shot time and your local best. The same numbers live on the Results tab. A short ring flashes on the crosshair for hits and misses.
  • Crosshairs: each game has its own profile, used in Train and the Range. Style (cross, dot, circle, T), color, length, thickness, gap, outline, center dot, opacity and an optional dynamic open-on-shot.
